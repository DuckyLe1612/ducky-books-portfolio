Business rule specification · Ducky Books

One rule, in full

How I write a rule before anyone builds it. I picked this one because every line can be checked against a published decree, and because my first version was wrong in a way worth showing.

1. The rule

Rule
A purchase invoice at or above the threshold, VAT included, must be paid by a method other than cash. Otherwise its input VAT cannot be deducted.
Legal basis
Decree 181/2025/ND-CP, Article 26, under VAT Law 48/2024/QH15.
Value and start date
5,000,000 dong, in force from 1 July 2025.
Who it affects
Every company on the system that records purchase invoices. Not sales.
Why it matters
Get it wrong and the company claims VAT it is not entitled to. That is found at audit, not by the books, because the books still balance either way.

2. What triggers it

Every purchase invoice just before posting, and again when the review screen opens. Three conditions, all required:

#ConditionWhy it is written this way
T1A purchase, not a sale.About the buyer's right to deduct.
T2Total, VAT included, at or above the threshold.At or above, not over: a five million invoice is inside.
T3Nobody has confirmed the payment method yet.A person's confirmation ends the check for that invoice.

The threshold is read by the invoice's date, not today's. An invoice from before 1 July 2025 uses the value that applied then. One function answers "what was the threshold on this date", and all the software calls it; 5,000,000 is written in one place only, a table with its start date and its decree.

Deliberate: a second copy drifts apart the first time the law changes, and nobody finds out until a return is wrong.

3. What the software does

It does not decide. It sorts each invoice into one of six groups by strength of evidence and shows the person the result. First matching row wins.

OrderGroupWhat was foundWhat it means
1BothCash payment and bank payment.Open it. Something is not right.
2Cash presentPaid out of the cash account.Deduction lost.
3Bank statementPosted statement line paying it in full.Strongest evidence: the bank's own record.
4Bank entryPaid out of the bank account, no cash.Good, weaker than a statement line.
5Not through the tillAllocated, not through cash or bank.Probably a debt offset. Needs a person.
6Nothing linkedNo payment document.Unknown, not innocent. Check when the document exists.

4. Acceptance criteria

#Given, when, then
A1Exactly 5,000,000 dong, dated after the start date, no payment linked: group 6, not skipped.
A24,999,999 dong: does not appear.
A3Dated before the start date: the earlier value applies, even if entered today.
A4Paid in cash: group 2, and the reason names the cash account.
A5Confirmed by a person: leaves the list and does not return on its own.
A6Undo a confirmation on one invoice without touching any other.
A7Only a person sets confirmation, never the software.

5. Edge cases, and how each was found

All three came from real books, not from thinking about it.

#CaseWhat went wrong
E1Settled straight from the bank statement screen. That path creates no allocation record. The check looked only at allocations, so a paid invoice was reported unpaid.
E2Marked "pay now" in cash while being posted. Its entry credits the cash account with no allocation, so it landed in group 6, not group 2. The cash guard was open where it matters most.
E3Several small purchases, one supplier, one day. The related rule for purchases with no invoice adds up one day per supplier. This one does not. Two rules, two rounding points, and readers assume they match.

6. The version that was wrong

The first design asked the wrong question. It read stored warnings instead of asking each invoice its condition now. A warning never written, or written before a payment changed, is no warning.

On the real books it left 1,341 invoices with no warning, covering 25.34 billion dong. Worst company: 523 invoices inside the rule, 120 warned, 403 silent, 21.14 billion dong.

Worst kind of defect, because the screen looked calm. A guard that does not fire is worse than no guard: people stop looking.

What replaced it. The check asks the invoice's condition every time the screen opens. Slower. It cannot go stale.

7. What the rule may not do

It may not confirm an invoice, post anything, or block a person from posting one they have decided about.

Not technical: whoever signs the return carries the liability, so they decide. The software only puts the evidence in front of them first.

8. When this specification stops being valid

When the decree or threshold changes, or a new payment path appears that the six groups do not cover. A new threshold does not replace the old: it is added with its own start date, and older periods keep their old value.
