The acceptance test script

5,987 automated tests were passing, and still not enough. Tests prove the software does what it was told, not that a day’s work finishes or the numbers are right. This script adds 32 steps, run on real books.

Written on 2 August 2026. This is the blank script itself: nobody has filled in a date, a result or a signature yet.

Who runs it. The chief accountant, on a client's real books. Not me: the builder should be the last to judge whether it works.

The software went into real use on 29 May, the day after I started it, and grew screen by screen as she used it. Nine weeks later, the fact that she had been using it was no longer proof, on its own, that it worked. A test script written before the code exists can only test a guess about how the software should behave; this one, written after nine weeks of real use, tests what was actually built.

A signed result would only show one company passing on one day.

How steps are graded

GradeMeans
PassFinished, figures match, no help needed.
Pass with frictionFinished, but hard, slow or confusing. Say which.
FailNot done, figures wrong, or help needed.

One fail in Group A or B stops the release: the update does not go out until it is fixed. Steps marked [POSTS] file the transaction into the books, changing real data, so the run starts with a backup.

Group A · One invoice (blocks release)

#TaskPass criterion
A1Load a sales invoice (an XML file, the digital form it arrives in)Listed; every amount matches the PDF copy, down to the dong (Vietnam's currency).
A2Load it againDuplicate reported; no second invoice.
A3Open invoice detailShows number, series, date, buyer, tax number, line items, tax rate, total.
A4Post it [POSTS]Journal entry, debits equal credits, dated invoice date.
A5Press Post againNo second entry.
A6Undo that entry [POSTS]Entry gone, invoice unposted. Activity log records both.
A7Repost against a project [POSTS]Project costing shows revenue and cost.

Group B · Cash, receivables, inventory (blocks release)

#TaskPass criterion
B1Raise a cash receipt [POSTS]Cash book rises correctly; the receipt required by law prints.
B2Print that receiptActivity log records the print.
B3Load a month of bank statementLines read equals lines in file; no line swallowed into a zero.
B4Match a receipt to an invoice [POSTS]Invoice moves to Paid; receivable falls correctly.
B5Open ReceivablesTotal equals sum of unpaid invoices; a customer opens to each invoice.
B6Add a stock itemCost-of-sales account fills in from its stock category, but can still be edited by hand.
B7Change its stock category to Finished goodsCost-of-sales account updates too, instead of being left outdated.
B8Receive stock on a purchase invoice [POSTS]Stock rises by the right quantity; value matches invoice.
B9Issue more stock than on handWarns immediately, not silently.
B10Run period-end cost transfer [POSTS]Cost-of-sales entry matches stock movement report.
B11Issue stock until an item's balance goes negativeBlocked until a written reason.

B11 is not a technical rule: on 31 July I gave the chief accountant three ways to handle negative stock, and she chose this one.

Group C · Reports and tax returns (recorded, does not block)

#TaskPass criterion
C1Open OverviewFive figures within two seconds. Amber reminder if cost transfer not run.
C2Open trial balanceDebits equal credits; opening balances match last year's close.
C3Export general ledger to ExcelFile opens; activity log records which report.
C4Export financial statementsTotal assets equal equity plus liabilities; legal citation in footer.
C5Export VAT returnFigures match the invoice listing for that period.
C6Open it in the real government filing softwareAccepted, no format error.
C7Export purchase listing for company taxEvery required column present; the two forbidden ones absent.

Group D · Running it, and failures (recorded, does not block)

#TaskPass criterion
D1Press Back up nowNew file in backup folder, not empty.
D2Press Rehearse a restoreReports a pass, with tables and rows checked.
D3Switch company and backNo figures carry over from one company to the other; the company name on screen always matches the figures shown.
D4Open it on a phoneTables scroll sideways, text is readable, buttons are big enough to tap.
D5Pull the network cable, press a posting buttonPlain message in the user's language, not an English crash screen.
D6Edit one item in two tabsSecond tab stopped, told to reload; first edit not silently overwritten.
D7Log in as staffOnly assigned clients visible. Owner-only pages refused with a reason, not a blank screen.

When this script expires

Run it again on any change to posting, inventory or tax code, database structure, a tax circular (an official tax rule) or statutory form, the machine it runs on, or a restore from backup.

A result belongs to exactly one version.

Why show this, not describe it. Before anyone could argue about it, someone had to decide what "working" means, write it down so someone else could check it, and record whose decision it was. That is the other half of the job: the half the role is for.
