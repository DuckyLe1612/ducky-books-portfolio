Business rule spec · Ducky Books

One rule, in full

In plain words: pay a big purchase in cash, and the company cannot claim back the tax on it. Below is that rule, written exactly enough to build and test.

1. The rule

Rule
If a purchase invoice at or above the threshold is paid in cash, the buyer cannot claim back the VAT on it. VAT is a tax added to the price.
Legal basis
Decree 181/2025/ND-CP, Article 26, under VAT Law 48/2024/QH15.
Threshold
5,000,000 dong, in force from 1 July 2025.
Why it matters
The books still add up when this goes wrong, so only an audit would catch it.

2. What triggers it

Checked on every purchase invoice, before posting and each time the review screen opens. All three must be true:

#ConditionWhy written this way
T1A purchase, not a sale.Only the buyer on a purchase invoice can deduct VAT; a sales invoice carries no such risk.
T2Total with VAT at or above the threshold.Exactly 5,000,000 dong counts.
T3The invoice does not say it was paid by bank transfer, and no one has confirmed how it was paid.Confirmation ends the check.

The threshold is read by the invoice's date, not today's. It sits in one table with its start date and decree. Only one copy exists, so there is no second one to fall out of date.

3. What the software does

Each invoice goes into the first group that matches. Cash comes first, because cash is what loses the deduction. Then payment evidence, strongest first. It is checked fresh every time, so the answer cannot go out of date.

OrderGroupWhat was foundWhat it means
1BothCash and bank payment.Open it. Something is wrong.
2Cash presentFrom the cash account.VAT deduction lost.
3Bank statementThe bank's own statement shows the full payment.The strongest proof.
4Bank paymentThe books show a bank payment, but the bank statement does not confirm the full amount.Weaker proof.
5OffsetMarked as paid, but with no cash or bank payment.Probably settled against money the seller owed us. A person should check it.
6Nothing linkedNo payment document.Unknown, not innocent. Review it again once a payment document appears.

4. Acceptance criteria

The tests it must pass before the rule counts as finished.

#Given, when, then
A1Exactly 5,000,000 dong, after the start date, nothing linked: group 6, not skipped.
A24,999,999 dong: not listed.
A3Before the start date: earlier value applies, even if entered today.
A4Paid in cash: group 2, reason names the cash account.
A5Confirmed by a person: leaves the list, never returns on its own.
A6Undo one confirmation, no other invoice touched.
A7By default only a person confirms. An optional setting, off unless the chief accountant turns it on, lets the software confirm when the bank statement shows full payment.

5. Edge cases it must handle

Both come from real books, and both are handled.

#CaseWhat the check must do
E1Settled from the bank statement screen. No payment link is written here, so the check must also read the bank statement. Otherwise a paid invoice shows as unpaid.
E2Marked "pay now" in cash at posting. The cash account goes down but no payment link is written, so the check must also read the cash account. Otherwise it misses the very case the rule is for.

6. What it may not do

It may not post anything. It confirms an invoice only through that optional setting. Only the chief accountant's account can turn that setting on.

7. When this stops applying

When the decree or the threshold changes, or when a payment path appears that the six groups do not cover.
