The user acceptance test script, as written

5,987 automated tests were passing when this was written, and it was still not enough. Tests prove the software does what it was told. They do not tell the accountant whether a day’s work can be finished, or whether the numbers are right. This script asks that instead: 32 steps, on real books, run by her, not me.

Translated from the Vietnamese original, committed 2 August 2026 at commit c1b6441. Reproduced as written: blank, not a completed run.

Why it exists. Those are different questions, and they need different evidence.

Who runs it. The chief accountant, on real data from a company she is currently keeping books for. Not me. The person who built it is the last person who should be deciding whether it works.

The dates do not line up the way a textbook project would, which is worth saying. The software went into real use on 29 May, the day after I started it, and grew a screen at a time as the accountant used each one. This script came nine weeks later, when “she has been using it” no longer answered “does it work”. A script written before any code tests a guess; this one tests what was built.

The blank record block at the end is the form the person running it fills in, and it stays blank on this page. A signed result would tell you one company passed on one day; the script tells you what I thought was worth checking before anyone could call it ready.

How each step is graded

Every step has a measurable criterion. None is graded on whether it "seemed fine".

GradeMeans
PassFinished, the figures match the stated criterion, and no one had to be asked.
Pass with frictionFinished, but something was hard, slow or confusing. Say which.
FailCould not be done, or the figures are wrong, or someone else had to be asked.

One fail in Group A or B stops the release. Groups C and D do not block, but every result is recorded. A step marked [POSTS] changes real data, so the run starts with a backup.

Group A · The life of one invoice (blocks release)

#TaskPass criterion
A1Load one sales invoice XML fileThe invoice appears in the list and every amount matches the PDF to the dong.
A2Load the same file againThe software reports a duplicate and does not create a second invoice.
A3Open the invoice detailNumber, series, date, buyer, tax number, every line item, tax rate and total all present.
A4Post that invoice [POSTS]The entry appears in the journal, total debits equal total credits, dated the invoice date.
A5Press Post again on the same invoiceNo second entry is created.
A6Undo the entry just posted [POSTS]The entry disappears and the invoice returns to unposted. The activity log records both the posting and the undo.
A7Post it again against a project [POSTS]Project costing shows the revenue and the cost against that project.

Group B · Cash, receivables, inventory (blocks release)

#TaskPass criterion
B1Raise a cash receipt [POSTS]The cash book increases by the right amount and the statutory receipt form prints.
B2Print that receiptThe activity log records that the receipt was printed.
B3Load one month of bank statementLines read equals lines in the file, and no line is swallowed into a zero.
B4Match one receipt against an invoice [POSTS]The invoice moves to Paid and the receivable falls by the right amount.
B5Open ReceivablesThe total equals the sum of unpaid invoices. One customer can be opened to see each invoice.
B6Add a new stock itemThe cost-of-sales account fills itself in from the group chosen, and can still be changed by hand.
B7Change that item's group to Finished goodsThe cost-of-sales account changes with it; it does not sit there stale.
B8Receive stock from a purchase invoice [POSTS]Stock on hand rises by the right quantity and the value matches the invoice.
B9Issue more stock than is on handThe software warns immediately. It does not pass it in silence.
B10Run the period-end cost transfer [POSTS]The cost-of-sales entry appears and matches the stock movement report.
B11Try the transfer while a stock item is negativeBlocked, and a written reason is required before it will proceed.

B11 is not a technical rule. It is the option the chief accountant chose on 31 July. I put three ways of handling negative stock in front of her, and she picked this one. The document records that it was her call, not mine.

Group C · Reports and tax returns (does not block, but is recorded)

#TaskPass criterion
C1Open the OverviewFive figures appear within two seconds. If the cost transfer has not been run, an amber reminder shows.
C2Open the trial balanceTotal debits equal total credits, and opening balances match last year's closing.
C3Export the general ledger to ExcelThe file opens, and the activity log records which report was exported.
C4Export the financial statementsTotal assets equal total equity and liabilities, and the legal citation is printed in the footer.
C5Export the VAT returnThe figures on the return match the invoice listing for the same period.
C6Open that return in the real government filing softwareIt is accepted with no format error.
C7Export the purchase listing for company taxEvery required column is present, and the two columns that must not appear are absent.

Group D · Running it, and when things go wrong (does not block, but is recorded)

#TaskPass criterion
D1Press Back up nowA new file appears in the backup folder, and it is not empty.
D2Press Rehearse a restoreIt reports a pass, and states how many tables and rows it checked.
D3Switch to another company and backNo figures bleed across; the company name on screen always matches the figures shown.
D4Open the software on a phoneTables scroll sideways, text is readable, buttons can be hit.
D5Pull the network cable, then press a posting buttonA plain-language error in the user's own language. Not an English crash screen.
D6Open two tabs and edit the same item in bothThe second person is stopped and told to reload. The first edit is not silently overwritten.
D7Log in as a staff accountOnly assigned clients are visible. Owner-only pages are refused with an explanation, not a blank screen.

When this script stops being valid

Run it again whenever any of these happens. The posting, inventory or tax code changes. The database structure changes. A tax circular or statutory form changes. It moves to another machine. It is restored from a backup.

A result belongs to exactly one version. The commit goes in the box above, and an old result is never reused for a new build.

Why I am showing this rather than describing it. Everything else on my main page is a screenshot of software. This is the other half of the job, and the half the role is actually for: deciding what "working" means before anyone can argue about it, writing it down where someone else can check it, and naming the person who made the decision that was not mine.
