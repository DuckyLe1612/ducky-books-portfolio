Business rule spec · Ducky Books

One rule, in full

How I write a rule before anyone builds it. I picked this one because my first version was wrong.

1. The rule

Rule
A purchase invoice at or above the threshold, VAT included, must not be paid in cash, or its input VAT cannot be deducted.
Legal basis
Decree 181/2025/ND-CP, Article 26, under VAT Law 48/2024/QH15.
Value and start date
5,000,000 dong, in force from 1 July 2025.
Who it affects
Every company recording purchase invoices. Not sales.
Why it matters
Get it wrong and the company claims VAT it is not entitled to. That is found at audit, not in the books: they balance either way.

2. What triggers it

Every purchase invoice, before posting and when the review screen opens. All three required:

#ConditionWhy written this way
T1A purchase, not a sale.Buyer's right to deduct.
T2Total with VAT at or above the threshold.At or above, not over: five million is inside.
T3No confirmed payment method yet.Confirmation ends the check.

The threshold is read by the invoice's date, not today's. One function answers "what was the threshold on this date"; 5,000,000 sits in one table with its start date and decree.

Deliberate: a second copy would drift when the law changes, unnoticed until a return is wrong.

3. What the software does

Six groups by strength of evidence. First matching row wins.

OrderGroupWhat was foundWhat it means
1BothCash and bank payment.Open it. Something is wrong.
2Cash presentFrom the cash account.Deduction lost.
3Bank statementStatement line pays it in full.The bank's own record.
4Bank entryFrom the bank account, no cash.Weaker than a statement line.
5Not through the tillAllocated, not cash or bank.Probably a debt offset. Needs a person.
6Nothing linkedNo payment document.Unknown, not innocent. Check when the document exists.

4. Acceptance criteria

#Given, when, then
A1Exactly 5,000,000 dong, after the start date, nothing linked: group 6, not skipped.
A24,999,999 dong: not listed.
A3Before the start date: earlier value applies, even if entered today.
A4Paid in cash: group 2, reason names the cash account.
A5Confirmed by a person: leaves the list, never returns on its own.
A6Undo one confirmation, no other invoice touched.
A7Only a person confirms, never the software.

5. Edge cases

From real books, not from thought.

#CaseWhat went wrong
E1Settled from the bank statement screen. No allocation written, and the check read only allocations: a paid invoice reported unpaid.
E2Marked "pay now" in cash at posting. Credits cash with no allocation: group 6, not group 2. The cash guard was open where it matters most.
E3Several small purchases, one supplier, one day. The rule for purchases with no invoice sums one day per supplier. This one does not. Two rules, two rounding points, assumed to match.

6. The version that was wrong

The first design asked the wrong question. It read stored warnings instead of asking the invoice its condition now.

On the real books: 1,341 invoices unwarned, 25.34 billion dong. Worst company: 523 inside the rule, 120 warned, 403 silent, 21.14 billion dong.

What replaced it. The condition is asked fresh each time. Slower. It cannot go stale.

7. What it may not do

It may not confirm an invoice, post anything, or block a person from posting one they have decided about.

Not technical: whoever signs the return carries the liability, so they decide; the software only puts the evidence in front of them.

8. When this stops applying

When the decree or threshold changes, or a payment path appears the six groups do not cover.
