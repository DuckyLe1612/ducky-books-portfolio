Six numbers, six queries

Read only, against the working database, 5 September 2026. Nothing sampled, nothing scaled up.

An earlier version did not balance. Its result line said 42,655 invoices; the three source counts underneath added to 42,621. Thirty-four unexplained, on the page built for checking. The portal count was stale: 10,370 where the table now holds 10,404.

The same version printed 415,380 actions above a query filtering out scheduled jobs: the unfiltered count. The query returns 409,668. A number its own query does not produce, and it got past me.

Both fixed below; two totals are broken into parts, so you can add them up.

Why the queries. A number with its query is a claim you can argue with, and shows my definition.

SQLite, encrypted at rest, opened with query_only = 1: nothing could write.

1. Invoices by direction and source

Result: 42,655 invoices: 23,161 purchases, 19,494 sales. 32,158 from XML, 10,404 from the tax portal listing, 93 typed by a person.

SELECT direction,
       source,
       import_source,
       COUNT(*) AS invoices
  FROM invoices
 GROUP BY direction, source, import_source
 ORDER BY invoices DESC;

The headline "99.8% nobody typed" means everything not source = 'manual' with an empty import_source. Not "automated": that is what the column records.

direction is in the query because version one omitted it and I called them all supplier invoices. Wrong by about 19,000 documents.

Two cuts, one total. Source: 32,158 + 10,404 + 93 = 42,655. Direction: 23,161 + 19,494 = 42,655. Neither held in the previous version.

2. Ledger size

Result: 70,320 journal entries in 178,197 lines, across 43 client businesses.

SELECT (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM ledger_entries)                    AS entries,
       (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM ledger_entry_lines)                AS lines,
       (SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT client_id) FROM ledger_entries)   AS clients;

An entry is one transaction, a line one side of it against one account. Mine quoted one on a CV, the other in a portfolio, until someone checked.

3. Bank statement lines

Result: 23,671 statement lines. 1,239 of them matched to an invoice.

SELECT COUNT(*)                                              AS statement_lines,
       SUM(CASE WHEN settle_invoice_id IS NOT NULL THEN 1 END) AS matched_to_invoice
  FROM bank_entries;

The other 22,432 lines are wages, tax payments, transfers between the company's own accounts, bank fees. None has an invoice to match. "Every line" is the wrong denominator, but reporting 5.2% beats dividing by something that flatters.

4. Use of the software

Result: 409,668 actions by people, 29 May 2026 to 5 September 2026, on 94 of those 100 days. The table holds 415,759 rows: 40 from scheduled jobs, 6,051 with no user recorded.

SELECT COUNT(*)                                          AS actions,
       MIN(date(created_at))                             AS first_day,
       MAX(date(created_at))                             AS last_day,
       COUNT(DISTINCT date(created_at))                  AS days_with_activity
  FROM audit_logs
 WHERE user_id IS NOT NULL
   AND user_id NOT LIKE 'script:%';

NOT LIKE 'script:%' removes only the 40 scheduled-job rows. user_id IS NOT NULL removes 6,051 more, written before a signed-in user was attached. An earlier version named the small exclusion, not the large one.

Three numbers, not one: 415,759 rows in the table, 409,668 traceable to a person, 6,091 not.

5. Hours at the keyboard

Result: 105, 181 and 236 hours across June, July and August, on 23, 30 and 31 active days.

-- Gaps of five minutes or less between one person's consecutive actions
-- are counted as time at the keyboard. A longer gap is not.
SELECT user_id, created_at
  FROM audit_logs
 WHERE user_id IS NOT NULL
   AND user_id NOT LIKE 'script:%'
 ORDER BY user_id, created_at;

Not a single query, and I will not pretend it is. SQL returns the rows; the five minute rule is applied in code: SQLite has no window function for it here. The threshold is a judgement: five minutes counts as still working, six as stopped. Change it and the hours change.

6. What the accountant corrects

Result: 10,724 times a person changed the account the software proposed. 5,734 of them made one account more specific.

SELECT json_extract(metadata, '$.previous') AS proposed,
       json_extract(metadata, '$.next')     AS chosen,
       COUNT(*)                        AS times
  FROM audit_logs
 WHERE action = 'ledger.line.account_edit'
 GROUP BY proposed, chosen
 ORDER BY times DESC;

The total, ten thousand suggestions overridden, reads as a broken feature. Grouped by day, corrections fall on 75 days, three of them carrying 60.4%: 3,377 on 18 July, 2,100 on 23 August, 1,004 on 21 August. Ten days carry 82.4%. On 26 of the 75 it was under ten. Not spread out: a few sessions of one person cleaning up historical imports.

The expensive part was checking, which the log does not record: looking at a screen leaves no row. The fix stopped being "make the guesses better" and became "show what has already been checked".

What this page is for. Someone who does this work asks which rows you counted, which you left out, and whether you noticed the difference.

Column and table names are as in the schema. No real client names, tax numbers or money amounts appear here.
