Business rule specification · Ducky Books

One rule, written out in full

This is the shape I write a rule in before anyone builds it. One rule is enough to show the shape. I picked this one because you can check every line of it against a published decree, and because the first version I specified was wrong in a way that is worth showing.

1. The rule

Rule
A purchase invoice at or above the threshold, VAT included, must be paid by a method other than cash. Otherwise its input VAT cannot be deducted.
Legal basis
Decree 181/2025/ND-CP, Article 26, under VAT Law 48/2024/QH15.
Value and start date
5,000,000 dong, in force from 1 July 2025.
Who it affects
Every company on the system that records purchase invoices. It does not apply to sales.
Why it matters
Get it wrong and the company claims VAT it is not entitled to. That is found at audit, not by the books, because the books still balance either way.

2. What triggers it

Every purchase invoice, just before it is posted, and again whenever the review screen is opened. Three conditions, all required:

#ConditionWhy it is written this way
T1A purchase, not a sale.The rule is about the buyer's right to deduct.
T2Its total, VAT included, is at or above the threshold.At or above, not over. A five million invoice is inside the rule.
T3Nobody has confirmed the payment method yet.A person's confirmation ends the check for that invoice.

The threshold is read by the date of the invoice. Not today's date. An invoice from before 1 July 2025 is measured against the value that applied then. There is one function that answers "what was the threshold on this date", and every part of the software calls it. The number 5,000,000 is written in one place only, in a table with its start date and its decree.

That was deliberate. Write the number twice and the copies drift apart the first time the law changes, and nobody finds out until a return is wrong.

3. What the software does

It does not decide. It sorts each invoice into one of six groups by the strength of the evidence found, and shows the person what it found. The first row that matches wins.

OrderGroupWhat was foundWhat it means
1BothA cash payment and a bank payment.Open it. Something is not right.
2Cash presentA payment out of the cash account.The deduction is lost.
3Bank statementA posted statement line paying the invoice in full.Strongest evidence: the bank's own record.
4Bank entryA payment out of the bank account, no cash.Good, but weaker than a statement line.
5Not through the tillAllocated, but not through cash or bank.Probably a debt offset. Needs a person.
6Nothing linkedNo payment document.Unknown, not innocent. Check when the document exists.

4. Acceptance criteria

#Given, when, then
A1An invoice for exactly 5,000,000 dong dated after the start date, with no payment linked, appears in group 6. Not skipped.
A2An invoice for 4,999,999 dong does not appear.
A3An invoice dated before the start date is measured against the earlier value, even if entered today.
A4An invoice paid in cash appears in group 2, and the reason names the cash account.
A5Once a person confirms an invoice, it leaves the list and does not come back on its own.
A6A confirmation can be undone for one invoice without touching any other.
A7Only a person sets the confirmation, never the software.

5. Edge cases, and how each was found

All three came from running it against real books, not from thinking about it.

#CaseWhat went wrong
E1An invoice settled straight from the bank statement screen. That path creates no allocation record. The check looked only at allocations, so a properly paid invoice was reported as unpaid.
E2An invoice marked "pay now" in cash while it is being posted. Its own entry credits the cash account, with no allocation. It landed in group 6 instead of group 2. The cash guard was open at the case that matters most.
E3Several small purchases from one supplier on one day. The related rule for purchases with no invoice adds up one day per supplier. This rule does not. Two rules, two rounding points, and a reader will assume they work the same way.

6. The version that was wrong

The first design asked the wrong question. It read warnings stored earlier instead of asking each invoice its condition now. A warning never written, or written before a payment changed, is no warning at all.

On the real books, that version left 1,341 invoices with no warning, covering 25.34 billion dong. Worst single company: 523 invoices inside the rule, 120 warned, 403 silent, 21.14 billion dong.

The worst kind of defect, because the screen looked calm. A guard that does not fire is more dangerous than no guard: people stop looking.

What replaced it. The check asks the invoice's condition every time the screen opens. Slower. It cannot go stale.

7. What this rule is not allowed to do

It may not mark an invoice as confirmed, post anything, or block a person from posting an invoice they have decided about.

The reason is not technical. The person who signs the return carries the liability, so that person decides. The software's job is to put the evidence in front of them first.

8. When this specification stops being valid

When the decree changes, when the threshold changes, or when a new payment path is added that the six groups do not cover. A new threshold does not replace the old one. It is added with its own start date, and older periods keep the value that applied to them.
